### Research Design

- **Methodology:** Mixed-Method (Convergent Parallel Design)
- **Target Population:** Youth aged 20–40 years
- **Study Area:** Jhapa District, Province No. 1, Nepal
- **Quantitative:** Stratified random sample (~385), structured questionnaire
- **Qualitative:** Purposive sample (20–25), in-depth interviews + focus group discussions

### Abstract

This thesis argues that the Buddhist philosophical concept of *dukkha*, understood in its full canonical depth as encompassing three dimensions — dukkha-dukkha, vipariṇāma-dukkha, and saṅkhāra-dukkha — provides a philosophically rigorous framework for analysing the structural conditions of dissatisfaction in contemporary modernity. Employing a **mixed-method research design** that integrates Buddhist philosophical analysis with quantitative survey data and qualitative in-depth inquiry among **youth aged 20–40 in Jhapa, Nepal**, it deploys the Buddhist causal analysis through *paṭiccasamuppāda* (dependent origination) and *taṇhā* (craving) to illuminate the mechanisms by which modern institutional structures perpetuate cycles of dissatisfaction — and demonstrates that this illumination is strengthened when grounded in the lived experience of a population navigating both Buddhist cultural heritage and contemporary modernity.
