### Abstract

This thesis argues that the Buddhist philosophical concept of *dukkha*, understood in its full canonical depth as encompassing three dimensions — dukkha-dukkha, vipariṇāma-dukkha, and saṅkhāra-dukkha — provides a philosophically rigorous framework for analysing the structural conditions of dissatisfaction in contemporary modernity. It deploys the Buddhist causal analysis through *paṭiccasamuppāda* (dependent origination) and *taṇhā* (craving) to illuminate the mechanisms by which modern institutional structures perpetuate cycles of dissatisfaction.
