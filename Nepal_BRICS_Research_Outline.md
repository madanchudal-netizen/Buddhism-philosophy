# Research Outline & Notes: Nepal's Strategic Navigation of BRICS Resource Diplomacy

## Article Details
- **Title:** Nepal's Strategic Navigation of BRICS Resource Diplomacy and its Impact on National Development
- **Target:** Multinational Enterprises Seminar – Peer Review Article
- **Period Covered:** 2015–2025
- **Methodology:** Qualitative case study with secondary data analysis

---

## Section-by-Section Outline

### Abstract (250–300 words) ✓
- Research question: How has Nepal strategically navigated BRICS resource diplomacy?
- Methodology: Qualitative case study, secondary data
- Key findings: Competitive leveraging strategy; measurable hydropower gains; structural risks
- Theoretical contribution: Himalayan Balancing Model; supplier-side resource diplomacy

### 1. Introduction (600–800 words) ✓
**Key points:**
- Nepal's geographic position: sandwiched between India (S/E/W) and China/Tibet (N)
- ~83,000 MW technically feasible hydropower potential; only ~2,400 MW harnessed by 2023
- 2015 Gorkha earthquake as turning point in Nepal's BRICS engagement
- NDB established 2015; Nepal joined BRI May 2017
- India = ~65% of Nepal's trade; dominant but coercive (2015-16 blockade)
- MNE relevance: BRICS SOEs as quasi-MNEs in energy sector

### 4. Analysis (1,500–2,000 words) ✓

#### 4.1 India — Dominant Partner
**Key Projects:**
| Project | Capacity | Developer | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arun-3 | 900 MW | SJVN Limited | Under construction |
| Upper Karnali | 900 MW | GMR Group | Agreement stage |
| Pancheswor Multipurpose | 5,040 MW | Joint Nepal-India | Pre-construction |

**Key Agreements:**
- Power Trade Agreement (PTA) 2014, amended 2021 (third-country trading)
- Integrated Check Posts (ICP) Programme
- Raxaul-Kathmandu Rail Project (commitment, delayed)

**Strategic dynamics:**
- India's hegemonic presence but coercive vulnerability (2015-16 blockade)
- Competitive pressure from China improved Indian project terms

#### 4.2 China — BRI and Hydropower Counter-Presence
**Key Projects:**
| Project | Capacity/Value | Developer | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kerung-Kathmandu Railway | ~USD 3B | China Railway | Feasibility stage |
| Budhi Gandaki | 1,200 MW | CGGC | Disputed/Renegotiated |
| West Seti | 750 MW | PowerChina | Pre-construction |
| Pokhara Airport | USD 216M | CAMC Engineering | Operational (2023) |

**Key Agreements:**
- BRI Framework Agreement, May 2017
- Trade & Transit Agreement 2016 (Chinese seaport access)
- 9 priority BRI projects identified

**Strategic dynamics:**
- China's engagement accelerated post-2015 blockade
- Budhi Gandaki controversy illustrates domestic political complexity
- Pokhara Airport: debt sustainability concerns

#### 4.3 New Development Bank
- Established 2015, HQ Shanghai; initial USD 50B capitalisation
- Expanded membership by 2024: Bangladesh, Egypt, UAE, Uruguay, Ethiopia
- Nepal not a founding member; exploring membership/co-financing
- NDB clean energy mandate aligns with Nepal hydropower priorities
- Alternative to World Bank/ADB without governance conditionalities

#### 4.4 Brazil, Russia, South Africa
- **Brazil:** Hydropower management expertise; Embraer aircraft
- **Russia:** UN diplomatic support on territorial disputes; military equipment; scholarships
- **South Africa:** UN peacekeeping coordination; governance technical assistance

#### 4.5 Nepal's Strategic Responses
Four empirical patterns:
1. **Competitive Licensing/Re-Licensing** — Upper Karnali case (GMR vs. Chinese interest)
2. **Multilateral Institutional Anchoring** — AIIB, SAARC, BCIM memberships
3. **Sequenced Bilateral Commitments** — BRI Framework Agreement (vague, flexible)
4. **Domestic Political Coalition Management** — Party-China/India alignments used tactically

#### 4.6 Development Outcomes
**Positive:**
- Installed hydro capacity: 800 MW (2015) → 2,400 MW (2023) — 3x increase
- Upper Tamakoshi (456 MW) — completed 2021
- Northern district road connectivity improved
- Third-country power trading enabled (India-Bangladesh)

**Risks:**
- External debt: NPR 1.2 trillion (~USD 9B) by 2023
- Pokhara Airport commercial viability questions
- Environmental/seismic risks in hydropower
- Asymmetric power dependence (India transit, Chinese investment)

2. Formal Debt Sustainability Analysis (DSA >USD 50M threshold)
3. Diversify energy export markets (Bangladesh, beyond)

- Political risk management essential

## Methodological Notes

### Data Sources Used
- **Government Reports:** Nepal MoF Economic Surveys, NEA Annual Reports, Nepal MoFA Policy Documents
- **Multilateral Institutional Reports:** NDB Annual Reports, AIIB Annual Reports, IEA Nepal Energy Outlook
- **Academic Literature:** 35+ peer-reviewed sources (see full reference list)
- **Project Documents:** SJVN, NEA, NWEDC project overviews

### Analytical Approach
- **Primary Framework:** Qualitative case study (Yin, 2014)
- **Case Selection:** Nepal as "most likely case" for supplier-side resource diplomacy
- **Data Triangulation:** Government documents + academic literature + project-level data
- **Limitation:** No primary fieldwork; sub-national data limited

### Validity Considerations
- Multiple case studies within Nepal (Arun-3, Budhi Gandaki, BRI projects) provide internal validity
- HBM framework derived from existing theory + Nepal evidence = analytical transferability
- Debt sustainability claims based on government-published figures
